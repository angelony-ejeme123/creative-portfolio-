7 Simple Ways to Write Product Descriptions That Sell

Help shoppers understand the value of your product—and feel confident choosing it.

By Angel Chisom Onyejeme  ·  5-minute read

Self-initiated portfolio sample. Written to demonstrate blog writing and conversion-focused copywriting; not commissioned by a client.

A shopper lands on your product page, scans a few lines, and decides whether to keep reading. In that brief moment, your product description has an important job: make the item easy to understand, show why it matters, and answer the questions holding someone back.

That doesn’t mean every description needs big claims or clever wordplay. Clear, customer-focused copy is often more persuasive. Try these seven practical ways to improve yours.

1. Lead with the benefit

Features explain what a product has. Benefits explain what that means for the customer. A ceramic travel mug may have double-wall insulation; the benefit is that coffee stays warm while the outside remains comfortable to hold.

Include the feature when it helps shoppers make a decision, then connect it to a real use or result. Keep claims accurate and specific.

2. Write for one kind of customer at a time

A description becomes more useful when it reflects the needs of its intended buyer. Think about who is most likely to choose this product, what they care about, and what they might be unsure of.

For example, a backpack description for daily commuters might emphasize an easy-access laptop sleeve and comfortable straps. A hiking version would likely focus on fit, capacity, and weather resistance. Avoid trying to speak to everyone in the same paragraph.

3. Make the first sentence do real work

Start with the product’s clearest point of difference or most relevant benefit. Instead of opening with “Introducing our new insulated bottle,” try “Keep your water cold through a full day of errands, commutes, and workouts.” The second version gives shoppers a reason to continue.

4. Replace vague claims with useful detail

Words like “premium,” “high quality,” and “best-in-class” are hard to evaluate on their own. Support a claim with a verifiable detail: the material, dimensions, care instructions, origin, or a feature that distinguishes the item.

Specific information builds trust and helps customers decide whether the product suits their needs. Never add a performance promise unless the business can substantiate it.

5. Make the page easy to scan

Most shoppers don’t read product pages like novels. Use short paragraphs, descriptive subheadings, and bullets for details such as size, materials, compatibility, or what’s included. Put the most decision-relevant information first.

Good formatting is part of good copy: it helps a customer find an answer without having to work for it.

6. Answer the questions customers actually ask

Review customer messages, product reviews, and support tickets for recurring questions. Do shoppers want to know whether an item is machine washable? Whether it fits a standard desk? What comes in the box?

Answering those questions directly can reduce uncertainty before checkout. Keep policies, guarantees, and technical details consistent with the business’s official information.

7. End with a clear next step

Once shoppers have the details they need, make the next action obvious. A simple line such as “Choose your color to find the right fit” can guide them without pressure. The call to action should match what the page lets the customer do next.

Small edits can make a big difference

Effective product descriptions don’t rely on hype. They connect accurate product details to customer needs, make important information easy to find, and help people feel informed about their choice.

Start with one product page. Clarify its main benefit, add the details shoppers need, and remove anything that doesn’t help them decide. Then refine the rest of your catalog one description at a time.
